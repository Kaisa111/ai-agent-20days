Real World Assets (RWA) refer to physical or traditional
financial assets that are represented on blockchain networks.
Examples include government bonds, private credit, real estate,
commodities, and invoices.

Tokenization allows ownership or economic rights associated
with these assets to be represented using blockchain tokens.
This may improve settlement efficiency, transparency, and
accessibility for investors.

RWA products may involve several types of risk. Credit risk
arises when the borrower or issuer cannot meet its financial
obligations. Investors may lose part or all of their expected
payments.

Liquidity risk occurs when investors cannot easily buy or sell
the tokenized asset. A limited secondary market may make it
difficult to exit a position at a reasonable price.

Smart contract risk comes from vulnerabilities or errors in
blockchain software. A bug or exploit may cause assets to be
lost, frozen, or transferred incorrectly.

RWA products may also involve legal and regulatory risk.
Token ownership does not automatically guarantee enforceable
legal ownership of the underlying real-world asset. The legal
structure, jurisdiction, custody arrangement, and investor
rights must therefore be examined carefully.
